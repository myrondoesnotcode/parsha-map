# World around it — Numbers: verification notes

Wikipedia quotes come from the live article text (MediaWiki API plain-text extracts, fetched 2026-09-27). Cached copies are in `scratchpad/wn/*.txt`. Other cached sources, also in `scratchpad/wn/`: `kitchen.txt` (Kitchen 1992 via bible.ca), `gard.txt` (Gardiner 1911, archive.org OCR), `imjseti.txt` (Israel Museum PDF), `ut70.txt` (UT Austin EIEOL), `quban.txt` (Vostok abstract, Russian), `enc.html` (Encyclopedia.com). Scripture: Sefaria default English (JPS 2023 gender-sensitive), Hebrew verse numbering. Verse texts are in `wn/sefaria_num.txt`.

Chronology: Egyptian dates follow the low chronology used on Wikipedia (Seti I 1290–1279; Ramesses II acc. 31 May 1279, d. 1213). Hittite dates follow the **short** chronology (Hattusili III c. 1267–1237), which is the one consistent with Kadesh in 1274 under Muwatalli. Wikipedia also gives middle-chronology dates (Hattusili c. 1275–1245); UT Austin uses a higher one again.

## Reuse
- Within Numbers, used twice each: Huy's tomb (behaalotecha: Kush; shelach: Retjenu), Papyrus Anastasi I (shelach: Shasu giants; masei: road to Gaza), Ramesses II "plunders the mountain of Seir" (chukat, balak), Ramesses II's Moab reliefs (balak: Moab; matot: Dibon), Sheikh Saad stela (chukat, matot). Everything else is used once.
- Overlap with Exodus/Vayikra: **Nebwenenef** (korach) is the same appointment as tetzaveh's event, told from a different angle (the oracle, not the rings and staff). Seti I's Beth-Shean stela (bamidbar) belongs to the same year-1 campaign as beshalach's Ways of Horus event but is a separate record. Battle of Perire (matot) is the war Merneptah's stele (vayikra) celebrates, but a different record (the Karnak inscription's spoil lists). Emar (pinchas) was used twice in Exodus (destruction, 1187); here it is its 13th-century wills and daughters' inheritance, a different event, but the mishpatim description overlaps ("wills, adoptions"). Mursili II appears in four Exodus/Vayikra events; the two here (accession taunt, banishing his stepmother) are different events.
- Possible overlap with the parallel Leviticus draft (not yet written when I checked): the Leviticus pass fetched pages on Hattusili III, Tudhaliya IV, Tarhuntassa, Kurunta, Muwatalli II, Tukulti-Ninurta I, Adad-nirari I, Nauri decree (empty), Deir el-Medina. If it uses Hattusili's usurpation, the Bronze Tablet, or the Nauri decree, one side should change.

## bamidbar (1321–1279)
- **c. 1321 Mursili's enemies' taunt.** Wikipedia, "Mursili II": "In 1321 BC Mursili II assumed the Hittite throne…"; "He was greeted with contempt by Hatti's enemies…"; "Mursili records the scorn of his foes in his Annals: You are a child; you know nothing and instill no fear in me. Your land is now in ruins, and your infantry and chariotry are few. Against your infantry, I have many infantry; against your chariotry I have many chariotry… (Comprehensive Annals, AM 18–21)". https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mursili_II
- **c. 1290 Seti I's Beth-Shean stela.** Israel Museum, *Pharaoh in Canaan* catalogue no. 22: "The inscription opens (line 1) with the date: Year 1, 3rd month of Shomu (summer), day 10."; "Altogether, the king dispatched three divisions of his army to put down the revolt – the division of Amun to take the town of Hammath, the division of Re to recapture Beth Shean, and the division of Sutekh (Seth) to seize the town of Yeno'am". (It gives Seti I as 1294–1279; I use Wikipedia's 1290.) https://museum.imj.org.il/en/exhibitions/2016/pharaoh-in-canaan/pdf/seti-stela-en.pdf. Wikipedia, "Seti I": "ruling 1290 BC to 1279 BC"; "A stele in Beth-Shan testifies to that reconquest". https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seti_I
- **1290–1279 Nauri decree.** Wikipedia, "Temple of Seti I (Abydos)": "The Abydos Decree by Seti I at Nauri, carved into the Cliff of Nauri, declares that the temple's property, the gold mines, boats, goods, and workers, belonged to the temple and was then protected. This decree ensures that its resources were unable to be seized by magistrates and fortresses and that the workers were unable to be conscripted for labor or have their animals taken". The decree's exact year (usually given as year 4) is not on the page, so I span Seti's reign. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_of_Seti_I_(Abydos)
- Verses: 1:3; 1:52; 2:2; 1:49–50.

## nasso (1313–1279)
- **c. 1313 Horemheb reorganizes Deir el-Medina.** Wikipedia, "Horemheb": "He ruled for at least 14 years between 1319 BC and 1292 BC."; "Horemheb also reformed the Army and reorganized the Deir el-Medina workforce in his 7th year". Year 7 counted from 1319 ≈ 1313 (my arithmetic). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horemheb. Wikipedia, "Deir el-Medina": "…those involved in the administration and decoration of the royal tombs and temples." https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deir_el-Medina
- **c. 1311 Mursili banishes his stepmother.** Wikipedia, "Šuppiluliuma I": "Šuppiluliuma's last queen was a Babylonian princess possibly named Malnigal… The Babylonian Tawananna would survive a decade into the reign of Šuppiluliuma's son and second successor Muršili II, who would demote and banish her after an oracle confirmed that she was guilty of causing the death of the king's wife Gaššulawiya." 1321 − 10 ≈ 1311. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%A0uppiluliuma_I
- **1279 Seti I's wine labels.** Wikipedia, "Seti I": "ruling 1290 BC to 1279 BC"; "In a 2012 paper, David Aston analysed the wine jars and came to the same conclusion since no wine labels higher than Seti I's 8th regnal year were found in his KV17 tomb." (KV = Valley of the Kings.)
- Verses: 4:49; 5:13; 6:3.

Tutankhamun "ruled c. 1332 – 1323 BC" (Wikipedia, "Tutankhamun"). - **c. 1323 Tutankhamun's trumpets.** Wikipedia, "Tutankhamun's trumpets": "a pair of trumpets found in the burial chamber…"; "one of sterling silver and one of bronze or copper, are considered to be the oldest operational trumpets in the world"; "The instruments were likely played on a single note for military signalling". https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tutankhamun%27s_trumpets
- **1319–1292 Horemheb's priests from the army.** Wikipedia, "Horemheb": "While the king restored the priesthood of Amun, he prevented the Amun priests from forming a stranglehold on power, by deliberately reappointing priests who mostly came from the Egyptian army since he could rely on their personal loyalty."

## shelach (1332–1213)
- **1332–1323 Retjenu tribute in Huy's tomb.** Wikipedia, "Amenhotep called Huy": "The Northern section displays an Asiatic tribute scene where Retenu chiefs present horses, lapis, gold, silver, and copper which Huy is later depicted delivering to King Tutankhamun." Wikipedia, "Retjenu": "the Ancient Egyptian name for the wider Syrian region, where the Semitic-speaking Canaanites lived." Wikipedia, "Beit She'an": "When Canaan came under Imperial Egyptian rule in the Late Bronze Age…"
- **c. 1269 Dapur.** Wikipedia, "Dapur": "The siege of Dapur occurred as part of Pharaoh Ramesses II's campaign to suppress Galilee and conquer Syria in 1269 BC. He described his campaign on the wall of his mortuary temple, the Ramesseum in Thebes"; "Egyptian reliefs depict Dapur as a heavily fortified settlement with both inner and outer walls and situated on a rocky hill"; "Egyptian soldiers climbing scale ladders supported by archers". Wikipedia, "Ramesses II": first siege in year 8 ("By November 1272 BC, Ramesses was back in Egypt"), second "in his tenth year". Dapur's location (Syria per Kitchen; Tabor per others) is noted on the page; I say Syria, following the inscription's "in the land of Hatti" and Kitchen.
- **1279–1213 Anastasi I, Shasu "of four cubits or of five cubits".** Gardiner 1911, p. 1* (General remarks): "The composition contained in the first Anastasi papyrus was written certainly not earlier, but probably also not later, than the reign of Rameses II, the name of that king occurring in several passages". Translation: "The(?) narrow defile is infested (?) with Shosu concealed beneath the bushes; some of them are of four cubits or of five cubits, from head(??) to foot(?), fierce of face, their heart is not mild". Note: "'some were of four cubits or five cubits' (i.e. 6 foot 10 inches to 8 foot 6 inches) now refers to the Shosu, as with due allowance for Egyptian exaggeration it well may do". (The manuscript itself Gardiner dates to Sethos II.) Wikipedia, "Papyrus Anastasi I" has the same passage and calls it "a satirical text".
- Verses: 13:23; 13:28; 13:33.

## korach (1279–1200; no traditional year)
- Note text follows parshaDates.json korach note (Seder Olam 8 places it after the spies, no year).
- **1279 Nebwenenef chosen.** Wikipedia, "Nebwenenef": "He was appointed High Priest of Amun in Year 1, III Akhet of Ramesses II"; the king: "I set out for him the whole court, and the chief executive of the troops. There were repeated (before) him the prophets of the gods, and the notables of his House, who were in his presence. But he was not satisfied with any of them, until I mentioned your name to him." ("him" = Amun, per "as my father Amun favors me".) Ramesses II accession "31 May 1279 BC" (Wikipedia, "Ramesses II").
- **c. 1267 Hattusili III.** Wikipedia, "Hattusili III": "c. 1275–1245 BC (middle chronology) or 1267–1237 BC (short chronology)"; "He served his brother Muwatalli II and his nephew Mursili III before rebelling and taking the throne." Wikipedia, "Mursili III" quotes the Apology (§10C): "…'Come! Istar of Samuha and the Storm God of Nerik shall decide the case for us!' Since I wrote to Urhi-Tesub in this manner…"
- **1203–1200 Amenmesse.** Wikipedia, "Amenmesse": "Various Egyptologists date his reign between 1202 BC–1199 BC or 1203 BC–1200 BC"; Krauss/Dodson view: "a rival king who usurped power sometime during Years 2 to 4 of Seti II's reign in Upper Egypt and Nubia"; "Seti would finally defeat his rival Amenmesse and return to Thebes in triumph"; KV10: "almost all of its texts and scenes were either erased or usurped by Seti II's agents. No mention of Amenmesse was spared." (Kitchen/von Beckerath instead have him usurp before Seti II; hence "probably".)
- Verses: 17:20 (= 17:5 in many English Bibles); 16:5; 16:3.

## chukat (1279–1213)
- **1279–1269 Kuban stela.** Wikipedia, "Wadi Allaqi": "A stela attributed to New Kingdom of Egypt Ramesses II discovered near Quban references the search and discovery of water for gold laborers. That well is located 60 km into Wadi Allaqi". Wikipedia, "Seti I": "Two important inscriptions from the first decade of Ramesses' reign, namely the Abydos Dedicatory Inscription and the Kuban Stela of Ramesses II". Wikipedia, "Ramesses II": "Ramesses II expanded gold mining operations in Akuyati (modern day Wadi Allaqi)." Krol, Kuznetsov & Ladynin, Vostok (Oriens) 2026 no. 1, abstract (Russian): the stela belongs to the start of his reign ("относится к началу его царствования"), and his officials come to him at Memphis to report the difficulty of gold-mining in the land of Ikait "из-за отсутствия колодцев на пути в этот регион" (because there are no wells on the way there). I could not find an English source for the usual "year 3" date, so I span the first decade.
- **1279–1213 Sheikh Saad stela.** Wikipedia, "Ash-Shaykh Saad": "An Egyptian stele, commemorating a campaign of Pharaoh Ramesses II, is locally venerated in a mosque as the "Rock of Job" (see below). The basalt monolith…"; historically Karnaim, capital of Bashan as Ashteroth-Karnaim. Kitchen 1992: "Like his father, Ramesses II also left a stela up in Bashan, at Sheikh Said to the north of Tell esh-Shihab." No year is known, so I span the reign.
- **1279–1213 "mountain of Seir".** Kitchen 1992: "In texts of his own time, Ramesses II twice describes himself as one 'who plunders the mountain of Seir with his valiant arm; in context, Shasu is used in parallel phrases."; "the mountain of Seir is already a fixed expression, reminiscent of the Hebrew phrase Mount Seir." Wikipedia, "Edom": "It was also called the land of Seir".
- Verses: 20:5; 21:18; 21:33; 20:18.

## balak (1279–1213)
- Seir: as for chukat. Wikipedia, "Edom": "The boundary between Moab and Edom was the Zered".
- **c. 1273 Shalmaneser I.** Wikipedia, "Shalmaneser I": "1273–1244 BC or 1265–1235 BC"; "In his second year he defeated Shattuara, king of Hanilgalbat (Mitanni), and his Hittite and Ahlamu allies."; "He was one of the first Assyrian kings known to deport his defeated enemies to various lands." The page does not say "Asshur" in Balaam refers to him. The significance only says Balaam speaks of Asshur taking captives.
- **c. 1270 Moab at Luxor.** Kitchen 1992: "Town (dmi) that Pharaoh's arm captured in the land of Moab: Btrt (Butartu)."; "The date of Ramesses II's campaign into Moab is unknown; it is probably best dated after Year 9, as recently suggested." (Year 9 ≈ 1271/70, so "c. 1270" really means sometime after c. 1270.) Wikipedia, "Moab": "…a colossal statue erected at Luxor by pharaoh Ramesses II… lists Mu'ab among a series of nations conquered during a campaign"; "Na'aman argued, however, that the identification of Mw-i-bw with the biblical land of Moab can no longer be upheld; the former was more likely well to the north. Kenneth Kitchen later responded to Na'aman, reasserting the identification".
- Verses: 24:18; 24:22; 22:4.

## pinchas (1300–1200)
- **1300–1200 Emar daughters.** Wikipedia, "Emar": "the tablets preserved at Emar, most of them in Akkadian and of the thirteenth century BC, are not royal or official, but record private transactions, judicial records, dealings in real estate, marriages, last wills, formal adoptions." "Property and Succession," *World Eras* (Gale), via Encyclopedia.com: "During the second millennium b.c.e., in some peripheral areas such as Nuzi in northeast Mesopotamia and Emar in Syria, a daughter was treated the same as a son in the eyes of the law and could inherit a share of her father's estate." (The page does not date this to the 13th century specifically. The span comes from the Emar tablets.)
- **c. 1282 Seti I names Ramesses crown prince.** Wikipedia, "Seti I": "Around Year 9 of his reign, Seti appointed his son Ramesses II as the crown prince and his chosen successor". 1290 → year 9 ≈ 1282.
- **1279–1259 Ramesseum calendar.** Wikipedia, "Ramesseum": "work on the project began shortly after the start of his reign and continued for 20 years." Wikipedia, "Medinet Habu (temple)": "The entire south wall is inscribed with a liturgical calendar containing 1470 lines of hieroglyphs. The Ramesseum of Ramesses II was used as a source for much of the text though that exemplar is now much damaged. The text is mainly lists of offerings to be prepared for daily, monthly, and annual feasts."
- Verses: 27:4; 27:7; 27:19; 28:3; 28:10–11.

## matot (1279–1208)
- Sheikh Saad: as for chukat ("It is kept in a village mosque": Wikipedia, "housed inside a mosque dedicated to Nabi Ayyub").
- **c. 1270 Tibunu/Dibon.** Kitchen 1992: "Town (dmi) that Pharaoh's arm [captur]ed: Tibunu. In this strictly Moabite context, this name is, and can only be, Dibon."; "First came Ahituv (1972)… wrongly locating Tibunu in Galilee". Kitchen's tone is polemical; the disagreement is real (Ahituv, Weippert, Cross are named as rejecting it).
- **c. 1208 Perire.** Wikipedia, "Battle of Perire": "fought around 1208 BC between the New Kingdom of Egypt, led by the pharaoh Merneptah, and a coalition of Libyan tribes and Sea Peoples."; "the Egyptian records describe the spoils in extensive detail, including even minor items such as arrowheads."
- Verses: 32:33; 32:34; 31:27; 31:32.

## masei (1279–1213)
- **1279–1213 Anastasi I, road to Gaza.** Gardiner 1911, summary: "(XX) Hori cross-examines his friend as to the places on the high-road to Gaza, and finds him totally ignorant of them."; text: "Come let me tell thee about the district of Buto of Sese… O Mohar, where is Raphia? What is its wall like? How many leagues' march is it to Gaza? Answer quickly!" (OCR shows footnote marks after "Raphia" and "leagues"). Gardiner: "The present passage describes the localities lying between the fortress-town of Zaru and the Philistine city of Gaza". Date: see shelach.
- **1267–1259 Urhi-Teshub flees to Egypt.** Wikipedia, "Mursili III": "After being deposed as king, Hattusili III exiled Muršili to Syria… Muršili later escaped and fled to Egypt… Hattusili III responded to this event by demanding that Ramesses II extradite his nephew back to Hatti. This letter precipitated a crisis… the two empires came dangerously close to war. However, both kings eventually decided to resolve the issue by making peace in Year 21 of Ramesses II." The span runs from Hattusili's accession (c. 1267, short chronology) to the treaty (1259). The exact year of the flight is unknown.
- **c. 1240 Bronze Tablet.** Wikipedia, "Hattusa Bronze Tablet": "a vassal treaty between Hittite King Tudhaliya IV and his cousin, King Kurunta of Tarhuntassa"; "The text includes a detailed description of the boundaries of the kingdom of Tarhuntassa."; "the only Hittite metal tablet discovered so far". UT Austin EIEOL lesson 7: "containing the state treaty of Tuthalijas IV, circa 1260-1240 B.C."; "it goes on at length to specify the boundaries of that land in detail." Wikipedia, "Tudḫaliya IV": "He reigned c. 1245–1215 BC." The date is soft: 1240 is the one year both ranges share. In the short chronology Tudhaliya would begin c. 1237, so the tablet may be a few years later.
- Verses: 33:2; 35:11–12; 34:2–12 (34:3 "from the wilderness of Zin alongside Edom"; 34:12 "along the Jordan and terminate at the Dead Sea").

## Things I was unsure of
1. The Kitchen 1992 chapter is read from a reproduction on bible.ca (an apologetics site). The text reads as a faithful copy, but a reviewer may want the printed volume (Sheffield Archaeological Monographs 7, pp. 21–34).
2. Kuban stela: only a Russian abstract and a Wikipedia sentence support the "no wells" detail. The date is a span, not the usual "year 3".
3. Bronze Tablet date (c. 1240) sits where two chronologies overlap.
4. Emar: the 13th-century span is for the tablets in general, not for a dated will naming a daughter as heir.
5. Pinchas' window (1300–1200) comes from the Emar span. Without Emar it would be 1282–1259.
6. Korach's Nebwenenef event repeats an Exodus (tetzaveh) event.
