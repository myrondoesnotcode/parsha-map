### devarim
- DEU-1 (wrong, summary): Deuteronomy 1:3 dates the start of the speeches to 1 Shevat; Seder Olam puts Moses' death on 7 Adar, 36 days later. Devarim is not his last day.
- DEU-2 (wrong, richContent.narrativeSummary): Neither figure fits. The eleventh month is Shevat; by the traditional count Moses died on 7 Adar, the next month, so he has about five weeks left.
- DEU-3 (misleading, richContent.narrativeSummary): The verse says only that the bedstead 'is now in Rabbah'; 'on display' and 'when Deuteronomy was written' go beyond it and assume a composition date.
- DEU-4 (misleading, richContent.didYouKnow): The Greek name Deuteronomion means 'second law', not 'second Torah'; the transliteration 'deutero-nomos' is also not the name.
- DEU-5 (unsupported, richContent.didYouKnow): No rabbinic source states this as a general principle. The Talmud draws a narrower distinction, about the curses only.
- DEU-6 (unsupported, richContent.historicalContext): No Akkadian farewell-address parallel was found or named; the Amenemhat text is a dead king's monologue to his son, which is a fair comparison only if described as such.
- DEU-7 (misleading, richContent.historicalContext): Deuteronomy switches between singular and plural 'you'; 4:1 addresses Israel in the plural (אֶתְכֶם, 'you' plural), while 6:5 uses the singular.
- DEU-8 (misleading, richContent.historicalContext): This is disputed, not settled. Excavations at Tell Hesban, the usual site of Sihon's Heshbon, found no clear 13th-century occupation.
- DEU-9 (wrong, richContent.jewishTradition): Devarim is always read on the Shabbat before Tisha B'Av, in midsummer; Nitzavim, not Devarim, is read just before Rosh Hashana.

### vaetchanan
- DEU-10 (misleading, richContent.narrativeSummary): The verse says only 'I pleaded'; 'many times' comes from midrash (Devarim Rabbah 11:10 says a decree was issued ten times), not the text.
- DEU-11 (misleading, richContent.narrativeSummary): There are several differences between the versions (e.g. 'false witness' שקר vs שוא, the order of house and wife in the last commandment); and Exodus also lists servants and animals among those who rest. What Deuteronomy adds is the Egypt reason.
- DEU-12 (wrong, richContent.didYouKnow): The Shema verse has six Hebrew words.
- DEU-13 (wrong, richContent.didYouKnow): Echad is the sixth word, not the second. The enlarged ayin of Shema is also omitted; the reason given is one tradition, not a documented fact.
- DEU-14 (wrong, richContent.didYouKnow): No anagram is involved (the letters don't match). The Mishnah simply interprets 'with all your might' as 'with all your money'.
- DEU-15 (misleading, richContent.historicalContext): This is one reading presented as fact. The phrase can also mean 'the LORD alone' is Israel's God; Rashi reads it as 'the LORD, now our God, will in the future be the one LORD' of all nations.
- DEU-16 (misleading, richContent.jewishTradition): The mezuzah also contains the second paragraph (V'haya im shamoa, 11:13-21); describing it as only the Shema and Ve'ahavta is incomplete.

### ekev
- DEU-17 (unsupported, richContent.didYouKnow): Sweeping superlative with no source.
- DEU-18 (misleading, richContent.didYouKnow): The alternative found in the commentators is naturalistic, not metaphorical: Ibn Ezra reports that some say it was a miracle and others that the Israelites brought many garments out of Egypt.
- DEU-19 (wrong, richContent.historicalContext): The 'resource curse' is the hypothesis that countries rich in natural resources such as oil have worse growth and governance; it is not the idea that prosperity breeds complacency. The opening claim about 'modern political theory' is also unsourced.
- DEU-20 (unsupported, richContent.jewishTradition): No source given or found for the post-Holocaust framing or the 'most important conversation' superlative.
- DEU-21 (misleading, richContent.jewishTradition): Tefillin rest on four passages, including 6:8 in Vaetchanan; 11:18 is one of them, not the source.

### reeh
- DEU-22 (wrong, summary): Debt release is tied to the seventh year, but a Hebrew slave goes free after six years of his own service, not in the sabbatical year.
- DEU-23 (wrong, richContent.narrativeSummary): Same error: the release of Hebrew slaves is after six years of each slave's service (15:12), not a seventh-year law.
- DEU-24 (misleading, richContent.didYouKnow): Superlative is unsourced; the scholarly identification is stated partly as fact; and the chronology is confused: the Book of Kings faults kings long before Josiah (Asa, Jehoshaphat) for not removing the high places.
- DEU-25 (unsupported, richContent.historicalContext): Linking the end of the Arad shrine to Josiah's reform reflects Aharoni's older dating; the current strata dating (Herzog's low chronology) puts the fortress sequence in the 8th century and the shrine's end is usually tied to Hezekiah. No source found supporting a Josianic phase.
- DEU-26 (wrong, richContent.jewishTradition): Rosh Hashana and Yom Kippur are not pilgrimage festivals and are not the subject of 16:16; no source links High Holiday attendance to this verse.
- DEU-27 (misleading, richContent.jewishTradition): The source (a Mishnah, not the Talmud) says 'the people' refrained from lending to one another; 'wealthy' and 'to the poor' are added.

### shoftim
- DEU-28 (misleading, richContent.narrativeSummary): The law does not limit the length of a siege; it forbids cutting down fruit trees even in a long siege, while allowing other trees for siegeworks.
- DEU-29 (misleading, richContent.didYouKnow): Solomon reigned centuries after Moses in the Bible's own chronology, so 'almost immediately' is wrong; the numbers are supported (JPS 1 Kings 10:26 renders פרשים as horses).
- DEU-30 (misleading, richContent.historicalContext): A scholarly thesis (notably Bernard Levinson's) is stated as settled fact.
- DEU-31 (misleading, richContent.jewishTradition): The prayer asks God to absolve the people and not hold the innocent blood against them; it is not a plea for vengeance.

### ki-teitzei
- DEU-32 (wrong, summary): Naso is the longest portion; Ki Teitzei has the most commandments (110 verses).
- DEU-33 (misleading, richContent.narrativeSummary): Only one baraita says so; Rabbi Yonatan in the same passage says he saw one executed.
- DEU-34 (misleading, richContent.narrativeSummary): Deuteronomy speaks of 'your fellow's' animal; the enemy's animal is Exodus 23:4.
- DEU-35 (wrong, richContent.didYouKnow): The causal chain is invented and reversed: the Mishnah (Berakhot 5:3) is earlier than the Gemara (33b), and 33b is not about the equal reward. The Gemara gives two explanations: the prayer breeds jealousy among creatures, or it makes God's decrees into expressions of mercy.
- DEU-36 (misleading, richContent.didYouKnow): One baraita says this; Rabbi Yonatan disagrees in the same passage.
- DEU-37 (wrong, richContent.jewishTradition): Honoring parents comes from the Ten Commandments, not Ki Teitzei. The quote conflates two Mishnahs: Ben Azzai (Avot 4:2) says 'run to a minor mitzvah... for a mitzvah leads to a mitzvah'; 'you do not know the reward' is Rabbi's saying in Avot 2:1; neither derives it from the mother bird. The link to the mother bird is in Devarim Rabbah 6:2.

### ki-tavo
- DEU-38 (wrong, richContent.narrativeSummary): The text has no 'six Levites'; Levi is one of the six tribes on Gerizim (27:12), and 'the Levites' proclaim (27:14). The Mishnah puts the priests, Levites and Ark below in the middle.
- DEU-39 (wrong, richContent.narrativeSummary): The curse section of Deuteronomy 28 runs from 28:15 to 28:68, 54 verses.
- DEU-40 (unsupported, richContent.didYouKnow): No source for this quoted reason. The Mishnah simply prescribes expounding this passage.
- DEU-41 (misleading, richContent.didYouKnow): Someone does take the aliyah. The recorded custom is that it is not split and that the paid synagogue attendant takes it (Maharil, cited in Mishnah Berurah), not that there is no aliyah or that the rabbi reads.
- DEU-42 (misleading, richContent.historicalContext): Joshua 8 is a text, not archaeology, and 'actual' asserts historicity.
- DEU-43 (wrong, richContent.historicalContext): Excavations date the Gerizim temple to the mid-5th century BCE (Josephus's later date is considered inaccurate), and current scholarship dates its destruction to about 111–110 BCE.
- DEU-44 (wrong, richContent.jewishTradition): Megillah 29a says nothing of the kind; this reading is Ramban's, on Leviticus 26:16.

### nitzavim
- DEU-45 (wrong, richContent.narrativeSummary): This speech is Deuteronomy 31:29, in Vayeilech, not Nitzavim.
- DEU-46 (misleading, richContent.narrativeSummary): The text says only 'today'; the timing to his last day is tradition (Seder Olam puts the whole of Deuteronomy between 1 Shevat and 7 Adar).
- DEU-47 (misleading, richContent.didYouKnow): The rule is that Nitzavim is always read before Rosh Hashana (sometimes together with Vayeilech).
- DEU-48 (wrong, richContent.historicalContext): Assyrian treaties also bound future generations: Esarhaddon's succession oath (672 BCE) binds the vassals' sons and grandsons 'who will exist in days to come'.
- DEU-49 (misleading, richContent.jewishTradition): Maimonides cites this passage (7:5) as the promise of repentance at the end of exile; 'built his entire' laws on it is overstated.
- DEU-50 (unsupported, richContent.jewishTradition): No source; the quoted Holocaust phrase (Emil Fackenheim's) is not a use of 'choose life'.

### vayeilech
- DEU-51 (misleading, summary): The verse says 'I am 120 years old today'; that this is his last day is the rabbinic reading, stated as fact.
- DEU-52 (misleading, richContent.narrativeSummary): It has the fewest verses, but V'Zot HaBerachah has fewer words and letters.
- DEU-53 (misleading, richContent.narrativeSummary): The comparison in the text is to Sihon and Og, not Egypt.
- DEU-54 (misleading, richContent.narrativeSummary): God's anger is future ('then My anger will flare up'), not 'in advance'; 'perhaps also a comfort' is not in the text.
- DEU-55 (wrong, richContent.didYouKnow): The last ceremony was Sukkot 2022 (5783), the year after the 5782 sabbatical year; also the State of Israel did not exist in 1945.
- DEU-56 (unsupported, richContent.historicalContext): No source found that Hittite treaties required reading to the vassal population (reports concern reading to the vassal ruler).
- DEU-57 (misleading, richContent.jewishTradition): In Ashkenazi practice the scroll lies flat on the reading table; it is held up for display (hagbahah).
- DEU-58 (wrong, richContent.jewishTradition): The call on completing a book is 'Chazak, chazak, ve-nitchazek', not Joshua's 'chazak ve'ematz'.

### haazinu
- DEU-59 (wrong, richContent.narrativeSummary): The poem is 32:1-43 (43 verses); 32:44-52 is prose narrative.
- DEU-60 (wrong, richContent.didYouKnow): Ha'azinu's layout (each line split in two, 70 lines) differs from the Song of the Sea's brick pattern; no other song is written 'this way'.
- DEU-61 (unsupported, richContent.didYouKnow): Unsourced superlative.
- DEU-62 (misleading, richContent.didYouKnow): The verse describes the eagle rousing its young, hovering, and carrying them on its wings; 'pushing from the nest' and 'catching them' are not in it.
- DEU-63 (misleading, richContent.jewishTradition): In years when Rosh Hashana falls on Monday or Tuesday, Vayeilech is read on Shabbat Shuva and Ha'azinu after Yom Kippur.
- DEU-64 (unsupported, richContent.jewishTradition): No liturgical use of Deut 32:9 was found.
- DEU-65 (unsupported, richContent.jewishTradition): No source for this reception claim.

### vzot-habracha
- DEU-66 (wrong, summary): Simeon is not named in Deuteronomy 33.
- DEU-67 (wrong, richContent.narrativeSummary): Simeon is not blessed; and 'shorter than Jacob's' is unsupported (Deut 33 has 29 verses, Genesis 49:1-27 has 27).
- DEU-68 (misleading, richContent.narrativeSummary): The valley is named ('in the land of Moab, near Beth-peor'); what is unknown is the grave.
- DEU-69 (misleading, richContent.didYouKnow): The restart happens on Simchat Torah itself, in the same service, not after it (the field's own jewishTradition says so).
- DEU-70 (unsupported, richContent.didYouKnow): The reason is interpretation stated as fact; the commentator found gives different reasons.
- DEU-71 (wrong, richContent.historicalContext): Mount Nebo is about 700 m above sea level.
- DEU-72 (wrong, richContent.jewishTradition): Moses's prophecy is the seventh principle; the thirteenth is resurrection. 'As a man speaks to his friend' is Exodus 33:11, and 34:10 is not the Torah's final verse.
- DEU-73 (wrong, richContent.jewishTradition): The burial is 'in the valley', not on a mountain; the reception claim is unsourced.

## Date bar

All ten speech parshiot use AM 2488 = c. 1273 BCE, 'Moses' last speeches, from 1 Shevat of the 40th year'; V'Zot uses AM 2488, 'Moses dies at 120'. Verified: the script's own chain gives Exodus 2448 (Nisan), so the 40th year runs Nisan 2487 to Adar 2488; 1 Shevat and 7 Adar both fall in AM 2488, which is winter 1273 BCE (3761 - 2488 = 1273). Deut 1:3 and Seder Olam Rabbah 10 ('from 1 Shevat to 6 Adar, 36 days ... died 7 Adar') support both events. Rashi on 1:3 supports 'shortly before his death'. No errors found. One side note (DEU-105): parshaList.json's approximateDateBCE 1210-1180 for every Deuteronomy parsha matches neither the scholarly (1300-1200) nor the traditional (1273) date.
